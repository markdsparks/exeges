# Reader Continuity Research: A Small Path Before More Study Tools

- **Date:** 2026-08-30
- **Decision supported:** D-006 / TASK-0005
- **Question:** What is the next smallest product outcome after question-aware clarification for a reader-first Bible app?

## External Signals

1. The [American Bible Society's 2026 State of the Bible report](https://sotb.americanbible.org/the-bible-in-america-today/) describes a growing group open to the Bible but unsure how to approach a large, complex book. It reports that engaged readers are more likely to read complete chapters or stories, follow a plan, and put reading into a regular schedule. This is association, not proof that an app feature causes engagement.
2. John Dyer's [research on digital Bible reader behavior](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197636350.003.0007) finds that the phone context changes when and how readers engage. For Exeges, that reinforces a quiet, text-led next step rather than a notification-heavy or feed-like surface.
3. [YouVersion's plan guidance](https://blog.youversion.com/getstarted/) shows that the market treats short daily selections and basic context as a common habit-building pattern. Its account, reminders, social features, and large plan catalog do not fit the current Exeges scope and are not evidence that Exeges should add them.

## Product Interpretation

The current reader can resume a precise location and can dive deeply after a meaningful verse. It does not help a new or inconsistent reader choose a coherent next chapter. The strongest adjacent outcome is therefore **continuity of reading**, not additional source breadth, a chatbot, more commentary tooling, a streak, or social accountability.

## Recommended First Slice

Offer one private, local **Read Mark as one story** path:

- one chapter at a time, in canonical order;
- an explicit “mark this chapter read and continue” action;
- a quiet next-chapter entry in the sidebar and at the end of the current path chapter;
- backup/restore support for the reader's private progress;
- no date schedule, streak, reminder, account, telemetry, social feature, generated devotion, new Scripture source, or change to ordinary navigation.

The design intentionally makes continuity easy without making completion or daily performance the product's center of gravity. Mark is a bounded 16-chapter Gospel journey, not a claim about a required reading order or a replacement for reader-directed navigation.

## Validation Question

In a moderated alpha, can a reader who is unsure where to begin start Mark, complete a chapter, return later, and know the next reading without losing freedom to browse or study elsewhere?
